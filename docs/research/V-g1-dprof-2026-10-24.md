**Reading note (an S0 vocabulary finding, recorded):** the chain line's "`N fwd`" counts **window
openings** (`vkc_dwin` increments only when the window was closed — a prompt forward closes it), so a
contiguous decode stretch prints `1 fwd` while its counters cover all 63 forwards. The divisor for
every per-token reading is the CLI line's one-row forwards (63), not the chain line's N. The
measurements themselves are complete; only the label undercounts. (If a future leg interleaves
re-prefills, N rises and the trap sharpens — divide by the CLI count, always.)

## 3. The rules, applied verbatim

- **R1 — `(A1 wait − A1 device) ÷ d_fwd` < 25 ms/tok ⇒ close G1 negative.** Measured: **5.49 / 5.58
  (a ×2) / 5.16 (b) / 5.53 (c)** ms/tok — a factor ~4.5 under the bar, in **both** config worlds, with
  the ×2 stability rep agreeing to 0.09 ms/tok. **R1 fires: G1 closes negative.**
- **R2** (25–100 ⇒ S1-only class): not reached — the term never leaves single digits.
- **R3** (> 100 AND leg (b) halves the same term ⇒ promote-gate slice): the premise dies at the first
  clause — no leg is > 100, and (b) does not halve anything (5.49 → 5.16, −6 %). The confirmation
  counters anyway: the tier's decode waits are **0.49 ms/tok** with promote on and **0.49** with it
  off; promote traffic exists (612 MiB over the window ≈ 9.7 MiB/tok ≈ 72 ms of 8.5 GB/s bus
  occupancy, ~41 experts) but **does not sit under the chain's fences** — H1 is falsified as a gap
  cause (there is no gap to cause). Side observation, recorded for the pool lane, not G1: `PROMOTE=0`
  is *faster* end-to-end (0.6577 vs 0.6077 tok/s; wall 1496 vs 1619 ms/tok) through tier placement —
  decode device-served share 22.5 % vs 18.6 %, 700 fewer queue-skips — a residency effect, not a
  pipeline one.
- **R4 — `(wall − block − chain wait ÷ d_fwd) > 500` ⇒ escalate (pool lane first).** Measured:
  **−191.2 / −192.3 / −186.5 / −202.3** ms/tok — nowhere near the bar; **no escalation**. (The slight
  negativity is the printed line's own denominator offset: wall divides by 64 generated tokens,
  block/head by the same 64 while the window holds 63 forwards, ≈ −1.6 %.)

## 4. The verdict

**ARTIFACT.** The ~196 ms/tok decode-window "fence wait without corresponding chain device time" that
the step-0 leg-diff produced is **method error, exactly the spec's H2**: the diff assumed equal prompt
waits (P8 = P24) across legs whose prefills diverged (512- vs 768-row chunks, live routing history),
and the prompt's whole-run wait mass (~17 s in these legs' own chain lines) leaked into the window
arithmetic. Measured directly, the decode window's A1 fence wait is **228.7–243.8 ms/tok against
223.5–238.3 ms/tok of A1 device time — a 2.2–2.4 % excess of ~5.5 ms/tok**, in the decode set *and*
the ttft set alike (the spec §2.2's cross-config bound, ~200–245 ms/tok with no gap, was the correct
reading all along). The whole gap budget — queue turnaround, fence signal/wakeup, tier interleave —
is that 5.5 ms/tok; there is no recoverable pool in the chain's fence discipline. G1's ranked slices
all close unfunded: S1 (settle reorder, ceiling 0–60 ms/tok by its own bound — the real term is 5.5),
S2 (frame merge), S3 (spin 2000→5000 µs, ceiling the same 5.5), and the R3 promote gate (H1 dead).

**What the legs did buy — the decode wall, decomposed by printed numbers for the first time** (leg
a-r1, ms/tok; the others are in the table): `wall 1619.5 = chain fence waits 235.0 (14.5 %) + tier
decode MoE CPU 821.9 (50.7 %) + forward glue 518.8 (32.0 %) [= block 1575.7] + head 72.9 − overlap
29.1`. The block decomposition closes exactly (`waits + tier CPU + glue = block`) in every leg. The
§2.4 "unattributed pool" is no longer a subtraction guess: it is **~1.34 s/tok of host-side work — the
tier's decode MoE CPU (~822, the routed-expert host compute at 18.6 % device share) plus ~519–720
ms/tok of forward glue** (h2d/cdn memcpys, host router reads, OMP wakeups, in-block sampling). By
R4's pre-registered formula there is no escalation; by the numbers, the pool lane owns everything
that remains — G1's ceiling (~5.5 ms/tok) is a rounding error against it.

**The next slice is the pool lane's, not G1's:** (1) the decode MoE CPU term — device-served expert
share at decode is 18.6 % (a) / 22.5 % (b) / 13.1 % (c) while the tier holds a 469-expert device
budget; `PROMOTE=0` beating `PROMOTE=32` on wall says the promote policy is moving the wrong experts
at decode (700 queue-skips under promote-on); (2) the forward-glue term needs its own instrumented
window (the CLI's block minus the chain's wait minus the tier's CPU is now a difference of printed
numbers, but the glue's interior — memcpy vs router vs OMP — is not yet split). Both are the manager's
next-window decisions; G1 recommends nothing further for itself.
